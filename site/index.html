Antivertising is a free browser extension. It doesn't block ads. It replaces each one with an ad for the advertiser's worst enemy, so the money spent reaching you works against whoever spent it.

How it works

Advertisers pay for your attention. This spends it elsewhere.

  1. It finds the ad

    Banners, sidebars and sponsored boxes are spotted as the page loads. The rest of the page is left alone.

  2. It works out who paid

    From where the ad leads and what it says. No pictures are read and nothing is looked up online.

  3. It hands over the space

    To the arch-rival where there is one. A Pepsi ad becomes a Coke ad. McDonald's becomes Burger King.

  • Some industries get no rival. Gambling, lenders and cosmetic clinics are replaced by the thing that makes them pointless, such as Keeping Your Money.
  • Unknown advertisers get heckled. If it can't tell who paid, the space says Not Buying It.
  • Nobody pays for placement. No brand named here is involved, asked or charged.
  • You can always undo it. Hover the black strip on any card to get the real ad back.
  • Nothing leaves your browser. No account, no tracking, no server. The brand list ships inside the extension.
  • The code is public. Read it, change it, add your country's brands.

Install

About a minute, on a computer.

Works in Chrome, Edge, Brave and Arc. It isn't in the Chrome Web Store, so you load it by hand. Not on phones, Safari or Firefox yet.

  1. Download it

    Download antivertising.zip

  2. Unzip it

    Double-click the file. You get a folder called antivertising. Move it somewhere it can stay, such as Documents: the browser runs it from that folder.

  3. Open the extensions page

    Paste this into the address bar. Browsers don't allow links to it.

    chrome://extensions

  4. Load the folder

    Switch on Developer mode at the top right. Click Load unpacked and choose the antivertising folder.

  5. Check it works

    Open the test page. It is a fake newspaper full of fake ads, and every one should flip.

To update later, download again, replace what's in the folder, and press the reload arrow on the Antivertising card. Don't remove the extension, or your score resets.

Questions

Fair things to ask.

Is this an ad blocker?

No. The ad still loads and the advertiser still pays for it. You just see something else in its place. If you want fewer ads, use a blocker. This is for when you'd like them to backfire.

Do the rival brands pay for this?

No, and they never will. Nobody named on a card is involved. The cards are made up by the extension, and each one says who originally paid for the space.

Why did an ad turn into "Not Buying It"?

The extension couldn't tell who the advertiser was. That happens most with ads that are only a picture. You can turn the heckling off in the extension's menu and leave those ads alone.

What does it know about me?

Nothing that leaves your computer. It keeps a score and, if you leave the option on, a log of ads it replaced. Both stay in your browser. You can export or clear the log from the menu.

A brand is missing, or matched wrongly.

The list is a plain file in the project. Open an issue on GitHub, or add the brand yourself and send it in.
